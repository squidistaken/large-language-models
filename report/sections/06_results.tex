\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{PEFT and Prompting}
\label{sec:results_main}

Table~\ref{tab:results} gives the test results of every system. In English, both fine-tuned conditions outperformed prompting the same model: PEFT-en reached a micro-F1 of 0.517 and PEFT-bilingual 0.511, against 0.428 for zero-shot and 0.425 for five-shot prompting, and the two fine-tuned conditions lie within each other's bootstrap intervals. In Croatian, PEFT-bilingual scored highest of the EuroLLM-9B systems (0.436), ahead of PEFT-hr (0.403), while PEFT-en (0.385) scored about the same as zero-shot prompting (0.380). Every system scored lower on Croatian than on English, by between 0.030 (five-shot prompting) and 0.132 (PEFT-en) in micro-F1.

Qwen3.5-9B outperformed prompted EuroLLM-9B in every setting and metric. With five demonstrations, it reached a micro-F1 of 0.513 in English and 0.444 in Croatian, close to the best fine-tuned conditions, although its Jaccard stayed below theirs. For both models, five-shot prompting raised or kept micro-F1 but lowered Jaccard. Only one answer on the whole test set was malformed, from zero-shot EuroLLM-9B in English (0.2\%).

The secondary metrics show how the systems differ. Five-shot prompting raised recall for both models in both languages, mostly at the cost of precision, and matched the full label set on only 9--14\% of lines, against 22--26\% for fine-tuning. Zero-shot Qwen3.5-9B was the most conservative system, with the highest precision and the lowest Hamming loss in both languages. Among the EuroLLM-9B systems, fine-tuning gave the lowest Hamming loss, and on Croatian, PEFT-en and PEFT-hr favoured precision over recall, whereas zero-shot prompting favoured recall. In Croatian, five-shot EuroLLM-9B scored above PEFT-hr on macro-F1 (0.386 against 0.380) but below it on micro-F1, so macro-F1 changes the order only of close systems.

\subsection{Seeds and Learning Curve}
\label{sec:results_curve}

Over seeds 42, 67 and 128 (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}), the standard deviations of the PEFT conditions were at most 0.018 in micro-F1, the largest being PEFT-bilingual on Croatian. The averages keep the ordering above with smaller margins: PEFT-bilingual still scored highest in Croatian (0.416 against 0.403 for PEFT-hr), a lead of about one standard deviation, and PEFT-en and PEFT-bilingual remained level in English (0.504 and 0.505).

On the learning curve (Figure~\ref{fig:learning-curve}), PEFT-bilingual scored above PEFT-hr at all five sizes, by between 0.006 and 0.069 in micro-F1. With 250 Croatian lines, PEFT-hr (0.343) fell below zero-shot prompting, while PEFT-bilingual (0.388) was level with PEFT-en, and with 1,000 lines PEFT-bilingual came within 0.003 of its full-data score. The curve of PEFT-hr is not monotonic (0.380, 0.365 and 0.416 with 500, 1,000 and 2,000 lines). Each point is a single run.

\subsection{Per-Emotion Results}
\label{sec:results_emotion}

The systems differed more between emotions than from each other (Figure~\ref{fig:per-emotion}). Anger and joy were the easiest emotions in both languages, and disgust was weak for every system, with an F1 between 0.15 and 0.38. In English, fine-tuning improved every emotion over zero-shot prompting, sadness most of all (from 0.26 to 0.49 F1 for PEFT-en; Figure~\ref{fig:emotion-gain}). In Croatian, the gains were smaller, with PEFT-bilingual improving six of the eight emotions by between 0.03 and 0.10 but not fear or disgust.
