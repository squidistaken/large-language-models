\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{PEFT and Prompting}
\label{sec:results_main}

Table~\ref{tab:results} and Figure~\ref{fig:metrics} give the test results of every condition with the primary model. In English, both fine-tuned conditions outperformed prompting: PEFT-en reached a micro-F1 of 0.517 and PEFT-bilingual 0.511, against 0.428 for zero-shot and 0.425 for five-shot prompting. The two fine-tuned conditions lie within each other's bootstrap intervals. In Croatian, PEFT-bilingual scored highest, with a micro-F1 of 0.436, against 0.403 for PEFT-hr and 0.380 for zero-shot prompting. PEFT-en scored 0.385 on Croatian, about the same as zero-shot prompting.

Every system scored lower on Croatian than on English, by between 0.030 (five-shot prompting) and 0.132 (PEFT-en) in micro-F1. Five-shot prompting changed micro-F1 little compared with zero-shot ($-0.003$ in English, $+0.015$ in Croatian) but lowered Jaccard in both languages. Macro-F1 gives the same best system in each language but reorders closer ones: in Croatian, five-shot prompting scored above PEFT-hr on macro-F1 (0.386 against 0.380), although below it on micro-F1.

The secondary metrics (Figure~\ref{fig:metrics-appendix}) separate the systems further. Fine-tuning gave the lowest Hamming loss, between 0.178 and 0.180 in English and between 0.199 and 0.201 in Croatian, against 0.216 to 0.250 for prompting. In English, five-shot prompting raised recall over zero-shot prompting (0.508 against 0.444) but lowered precision (0.365 against 0.413), the lowest precision of any system in English. On Croatian, PEFT-en and PEFT-hr had higher precision than recall (0.426 against 0.351, and 0.426 against 0.382), while zero-shot prompting showed the opposite (0.342 against 0.427).

\subsection{Prompting Both Models}
\label{sec:results_prompting}

Table~\ref{tab:prompting-test} and Figure~\ref{fig:prompting-test} compare the two prompted models. Qwen3.5-9B outperformed EuroLLM-9B in every setting and metric. With five demonstrations, it reached a micro-F1 of 0.513 in English and 0.444 in Croatian, close to the best fine-tuned EuroLLM-9B conditions (0.517 and 0.436), although its Jaccard stayed below theirs. For both models, five-shot prompting raised or kept micro-F1 but lowered Jaccard. Only one answer on the whole test set was malformed, from zero-shot EuroLLM-9B in English (0.2\%).

\subsection{Variation over Seeds}
\label{sec:results_seeds}

Table~\ref{tab:seeds} gives the mean and standard deviation of each PEFT condition over seeds 42, 67 and 128. Standard deviations were at most 0.018 in micro-F1, the largest being for PEFT-bilingual on Croatian. The averages keep the ordering of Table~\ref{tab:results}, but with smaller margins: PEFT-bilingual still scored highest in Croatian (0.416 against 0.403 for PEFT-hr), a lead of about one standard deviation, and PEFT-en and PEFT-bilingual remained level in English (0.504 and 0.505).

\subsection{Learning Curve}
\label{sec:results_curve}

Figure~\ref{fig:learning-curve} shows the Croatian test scores as the Croatian training data grows from 250 lines to all 3,803. PEFT-bilingual scored above PEFT-hr at all five sizes, by between 0.006 and 0.069 in micro-F1. With 250 Croatian lines, PEFT-hr (0.343) fell below zero-shot prompting, while PEFT-bilingual (0.388) was level with PEFT-en, and with 1,000 lines PEFT-bilingual came within 0.003 of its full-data score. The curve of PEFT-hr is not monotonic: it scored 0.380 with 500 lines, 0.365 with 1,000 and 0.416 with 2,000, above its full-data score of 0.403. Each point is a single run.
