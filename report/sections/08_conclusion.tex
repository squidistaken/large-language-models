\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

With the same model, EuroLLM-9B, fine-tuning clearly outperformed prompting in English, raising micro-F1 from 0.428 with zero-shot prompting to 0.517, and in Croatian the best result came from bilingual fine-tuning (0.436 against 0.380). Prompting gave too many labels, especially with five demonstrations, and much of the gain from fine-tuning came from learning how many emotions to report. Fine-tuning on English alone did not transfer to Croatian, but adding Croatian data to English helped most. Bilingual fine-tuning scored above Croatian-only fine-tuning at every amount of Croatian data, and came close to its full-data score with 1,000 Croatian lines.

Prompt wording mattered little next to the differences between systems, and five-shot Qwen3.5-9B matched the fine-tuned EuroLLM-9B without training, although it ran unquantised. All Croatian results, however, measure agreement with projected labels, some inherited from misaligned lines.

With more time, we would first have a Croatian reader verify the test labels, separating model errors from label errors. Beyond this, we see the following directions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Translate-test evaluation:} Classifying machine-translated Croatian test lines with PEFT-en would show whether the Croatian gap lies in the model or in the labels.
    \item \textbf{Filtering misaligned pairs:} Rescoring every system on the pairs that a multilingual sentence-embedding model judges well aligned would estimate the projection noise without a Croatian reader.
    \item \textbf{Controlling the number of labels:} Thresholding the model's probability for each label instead of generating a free-form list, and choosing demonstrations whose label counts match the data, would target the over-labelling behind much of the difference between systems.
    \item \textbf{Dialogue context and a second fine-tuned model:} Adding preceding subtitle lines would address ambiguous lines, and fine-tuning both models at the same precision would show whether fine-tuning still pays off on a stronger base model.
\end{itemize}

Comparing our results with those for other resource bands would, in addition, show whether the gap between fine-tuning and prompting depends on the amount of target-language data.
